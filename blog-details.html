The Ultimate Guide to Island Hopping in Bali & Nusa Penida: Secret Spots and Cultural Gems

Elena Vance Senior Travel Editor & Photojournalist • Oct 24, 2026

For decades, Bali has captured the imaginations of intrepid wanderers. But beyond the bustling beach clubs of Canggu and Seminyak lies an archipelago rich with ancient temple rituals, jungle ravines, and the wild, dramatic limestone cliffs of neighboring Nusa Penida.

1. Escaping the Crowds in Nusa Penida

Just a 40-minute high-speed catamaran ride from Sanur harbor brings you to Nusa Penida. Most day-trippers arrive at 10:00 AM and rush to Kelingking Beach (the iconic 'T-Rex' cliff). To experience this geologic wonder in peace, our Stackly guides recommend staying overnight on the island and arriving at the cliff lookout at 6:30 AM when the morning mist still hugs the sheer rock face.

Stackly Pro-Tip: Rent a private driver with an air-conditioned 4WD vehicle instead of scooters. The roads across western Nusa Penida can be rugged and narrow, and having an experienced native driver saves hours of navigational stress.

2. The Spiritual Serenity of Ubud's Sacred Waters

In the central highlands, Ubud is the cultural heartbeat of Bali. While the Sacred Monkey Forest is delightful, make time for an early morning pilgrimage to Tirta Empul. Here, underground spring waters fill sacred stone pools where locals perform the Melukat purification ritual. Participating with an elder guide who explains the offerings (canang sari) is a deeply moving experience that grounds your entire stay.

3. Marine Encounters: Swimming Alongside Giant Manta Rays

Manta Point on Nusa Penida's south coast is one of the world's most reliable year-round cleaning stations for Reef Manta Rays (Mobula alfredi). Floating calmly on the ocean surface while these gentle oceanic giants glide just feet beneath you with three-meter wingspans is an unforgettable testament to marine beauty.
